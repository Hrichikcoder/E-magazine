EDITOR'S NOTE SECURING THE NORTHEAST
FROM THE EDITOR

Editor's Note

When I first received this AICTE assignment — to compile a magazine on the Indian Army's role in Northeast India — honestly, I didn't know where to begin. The Northeast felt distant, something I'd only seen in headlines about unrest or in beautiful travel reels. But as I started reading, article after article, something shifted.

I discovered a region where a soldier might spend the morning defusing an IED and the afternoon coaching students for competitive exams. Where an Assam Rifles jawan who patrols the Myanmar border also distributes sewing machines to women in remote Manipur villages. Where the same army that extends AFSPA also runs Project Pranita to place young women in Marriott hotels for a better livelihood. The contradictions are real, and I've tried to present them honestly rather than flatten them into a simple narrative.

Curating these articles taught me things no textbook could. I learned that “security” in the Northeast isn't just about guns and fencing — it's about earning trust village by village, one medical camp, one peace dialogue, one surrendered militant at a time. I learned that a single jawan's sacrifice near Nampong reverberates through communities in ways that statistics cannot capture.

I have tried my best to verify every source, credit every photograph accurately, and present each story with the seriousness it deserves. If there are imperfections, they are mine alone. I hope this magazine gives you even a fraction of the perspective it gave me — that the Northeast is not a faraway frontier, but a living, breathing part of the India we are all responsible for.

Student Editor

Centre extends AFSPA in parts of Manipur, Arunachal and Nagaland for six months

The Indian Express | Multiple States
Photo: The Indian Express
The continued application of AFSPA aims to provide forces with the necessary legal framework to conduct counter-insurgency operations.

The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has officially announced the extension of the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958, in specific "disturbed areas" across Manipur, Nagaland, and Arunachal Pradesh for a further period of six months. The notification underscores the Centre's assessment that the security situation in these pockets, characterized by volatile ethnic dynamics and sporadic militant activities, necessitates the continued presence and specialized operational powers of the armed forces.

While AFSPA has been systematically rolled back from numerous districts across the Northeast over the past few years owing to an overall improvement in the security climate, areas deemed sensitive-particularly those sharing porous international borders or experiencing internal displacement and unrest-remain under its purview. Security agencies argue that the Act is vital for maintaining public order and neutralizing armed threats effectively.

Sevok–Rangpo Railway Project: Construction Through Difficult Himalayan Terrain

Press Information Bureau | Sikkim/West Bengal
Photo: Press Information Bureau
Engineering marvel: Over 80% of the railway line passes through tunnels, navigating one of the most challenging terrains in the world.

The Sevok-Rangpo Railway project, a marvel of modern engineering, is steadily inching towards completion despite the immense challenges posed by the fragile geology of the Himalayan foothills. Spanning a total length of 44.96 kilometers, the project is a testament to India's commitment to enhancing border infrastructure. According to the latest project updates, construction is approximately 80% complete, a remarkable feat given the frequent monsoons and seismic sensitivity of the region.

The intricate design includes 14 major tunnels and 23 bridges, ensuring that environmental impact is minimized while maintaining structural integrity. Once operational, the railway line will fundamentally alter the logistics and strategic mobility landscape of the region. It is poised to offer a reliable, all-weather transport alternative, thereby significantly boosting local commerce, unlocking immense tourism potential, and enabling swift military deployments to the strategic borders of Sikkim.

  • Completion Status: ~80% complete
